# Working idea — Acceptable-performance boundary

## Slide copy

Acceptable-performance boundary

**Monitoring sees a proxy, not the true functional limit.**

Diagram labels:

- Solid line — actual loss of acceptable performance
- Dotted line — perceived or marginal boundary
- Gap — intended error margin

**Crossing the dotted line enters the error margin—it does not mean failure has already occurred.**

What action restores recoverability?

## Speaker notes

The true functional boundary is uncertain. Monitoring can operationalize observable proxies and help establish a perceived boundary inside it.

Crossing that perceived boundary enters the protected error margin. The margin is exhausted only at the actual functional boundary.

Monitoring is a sensor, not the restoring force. A response appears only when the signal connects to interpretation, authority, and action: throttling work, reducing scope, rollback, adding capacity, or redesigning the system.

Do not describe corrective action as pushing the point back to the center. The objective is to restore sufficient margin and enter a controlled, recoverable operating regime.

Repeated dotted-line crossings without visible harm can have the opposite effect: the organization may move the perceived boundary outward and normalize the reduced margin.

Sustained workload pressure can weaken the safety counter-gradient by consuming the attention used to notice weak signals, investigate ambiguity, and strengthen response. This sets up the next slide's drift toward the workload–performance intersection.

Correctness, safety, security, latency, and behavior remain dimensions of acceptable performance. The point is that each is represented through negotiated, fallible indicators.
