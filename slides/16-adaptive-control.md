## Speaker notes

Keep the four audience themes tied to one model. Economic failure, unacceptable workload and functionally unacceptable performance constrain the feasible operating space. The error margin is the protected performance-side buffer, not an additional constraint. This closing arrangement is the talk's synthesis; Chapter 5 does not use Rasmussen's model.

Economic: evaluate cost per completed, verified, valuable outcome. Include the work transferred to reviewers, maintainers and responders, along with future coupling and recovery costs. Budgets and quotas guide control; they do not themselves define the actual viability constraint.

Workload: match admitted work to focused review capacity and contextual knowledge. Change scope, timing or demand when verification cannot keep pace. Queue growth does not alone prove a defect increase; risk additionally depends on adaptations that shed assurance.

Performance: evaluate people and tools together across good, mediocre and poor outputs, including convincing mistakes. Repeat appropriate evaluation as models, workflow, staffing and dependencies change. Operational signals remain imperfect proxies for the functional limit.

Error Margin: maintain usable signals, authority to intervene and practiced recovery. The margin does not act on its own. Timely interpretation and a capable response let feedback restore margin and recoverability.

Sense, Constrain, Adapt and Correct remain useful action verbs supporting these themes, rather than a second audience framework. Use AI to strengthen testing, diagnosis and verification where joint evidence supports it. The desired outcome is sufficient margin, bounded excursions and recoverability, with no privileged geometric center.
