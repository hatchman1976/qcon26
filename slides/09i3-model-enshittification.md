## Speaker notes

Doctorow describes a two-sided platform moving value from users to business customers, then taking more from both groups. Business customers mean advertisers, sellers or other participants, distinct from the platform owner. The headings are a paraphrase. Applying this pattern to AI is a risk to examine, not a prediction that every provider follows it.

### Lower costs do not determine who keeps the savings

Stanford’s AI Index article reports that the price of querying models at GPT-3.5-level MMLU performance fell from $20 per million tokens in November 2022 to $0.07 in October 2024. Its 2025 report also describes hardware costs per unit of performance declining by 30% annually. These are historical trends, not guarantees about future token prices or total workflow costs.

The cloud analogy concerns competition and switching. For its UK and EEA market definition, the CMA’s July 2025 cloud decision found concentrated supply, sustained AWS and Microsoft returns above their cost of capital, and barriers to switching. Its April 2024 foundation-model review identified risks from control over critical inputs and routes to customers. These findings support questioning how fully efficiency gains reach customers, rather than asserting an inevitable AI oligopoly.

The October 2026 CMA update records improvements, including removal of transfer fees for switching providers, while identifying remaining barriers. Cloud prices have risen for some services and fallen for others. The precedent is imperfect competition, not a claim that cloud prices never fall.

DeepSeek still supplies MIT-licensed model weights and charges for its hosted API. Open weights do not mean free compute. The evidence does not establish that incumbents will push DeepSeek out. The question is whether competing models remain practically accessible and switching remains viable as the market evolves.
