# Slide 12 — Team exercise

## Slide copy

Take this back to your team

**Do more than draw the envelope. Stress the control loop.**

1. Trace one AI-mediated flow from generation to verification.
2. Mark its economic, workload, and performance margins.
3. Mark each signal, its delay, and who can see it.
4. Pre-authorize action before assurance is shed.

Protect assurance by trading scope or latency—or adding capacity.

## Speaker notes

Use one real workflow. Draw the boundaries as perceived constraints, then mark the current operating-point kernel and its excursions. Do not pretend that the exact functional boundary is known.

For the economic dimension, mark three things separately: current usage and committed work; the team's budget, quota, or ROI guardrail; and any evidence that sustainable financial capacity has actually changed. Estimate total lifecycle cost per completed, verified, valuable outcome, including human assurance and rework—not only tokens.

Classify observations by their place in the sequence: review-load ratio, queue age, and work in progress describe demand and capacity; context switching and after-hours work are compensatory adaptations; falling review depth, bypasses, and deferred maintenance show assurance shedding; burnout, sickness absence, and attrition are later human outcomes. Burnout can warn of lost performance-control capacity when corroborated by workload evidence.

Finish with one authorized response that restores margin: throttle admission, reduce scope, accept latency, add capacity, strengthen verification, or roll back. The goal is a controlled regime, not a return to the center.
