# Slide 11 — Control-loop bandwidth

## Slide copy

Control-loop bandwidth

**A boundary cannot push the point back. A capable control loop can.**

- **Sense:** useful outcomes · token spend · arrival rate · queue age · performance
- **Constrain:** admission · WIP · token budgets · permissions
- **Adapt:** model routing · caching · batching · scope · validation
- **Correct:** rollback · escalation · learning · restore margin

**Use AI to increase assurance bandwidth—not only output gain.**

## Speaker notes

Make the 2024–2026 distinction explicit. The 2024 talk focused on the geometry of boundaries, margins, and drift. AI adds a temporal control problem: change-arrival rate and variability can grow faster than sensing, expert verification, and organizational response.

Monitoring is only a sensor. Feedback creates counterpressure when signals are timely and trusted and someone has the authority and capacity to act. A highly visible limit with no response authority does not repel anything.

Prefer paying in scope or delivery latency before assurance becomes the pressure-release valve.

Make the economic controller explicit. Measure total lifecycle cost per completed, verified, valuable outcome—not merely token price or generated volume. A token budget without outcome value encourages cheap waste; a throughput target without assurance cost encourages risk transfer.

Constrain demand before cutting verification: reject low-value workflows, cap agent loops and retries, route routine work to cheaper models, cache stable context, batch suitable requests, and reduce context size. These actions can improve economic margin without shrinking human adaptive capacity.

AI can strengthen the controller through triage, testing, validation, and anomaly analysis—but only when it produces trustworthy evidence rather than another review queue.
