# Slide 10 — Human Behaviour with LLM Tools

## Slide copy

**Human behaviour with LLM tools**

Rasmussen treats behaviour as part of control. LLM tools change the conditions in which people work.

Starting state:

**ESTABLISHED**

People adapt work to local conditions.

Those adaptations can maintain control or create conditions for failure.

Reveal 1:

**EARLY EVIDENCE**

Studies find changes in task pace and cognitive effort.

Effects differ by task and experience.

Reveal 2:

**OPEN QUESTION**

How do these changes affect judgment, learning, and feedback across the control structure?

Reveal 3:

**Rasmussen’s framework identifies what to examine. Work with LLM tools needs fresh evidence.**

## Speaker notes

Rasmussen treats human and organisational behaviour as constitutive of the socio-technical system. People and organisations are adaptive components and controllers within it, not an external human input. The paper argues that work migrates under local pressures and that control depends on objectives, feedback, competence, coordination, and commitment.

The established point is contextual adaptation. Locally sensible behaviour can preserve control, but interactions among adaptations can also create the conditions for failure.

Early LLM evidence shows real but uneven changes. A 2025 CHI survey of 319 knowledge workers found that critical-thinking effort shifted toward verification, response integration, and task stewardship; higher confidence in AI was associated with less critical scrutiny. A randomized study of 758 consultants found faster, higher-quality work on tasks within the model's capability frontier, but lower correctness on a task outside it. Other workplace studies report heterogeneous gains by task and experience.

The system-level question remains open. We need to re-measure problem framing, attention and verification, skill retention under pressure, communication of intent, authority, and feedback fidelity across the control hierarchy. Current task-level studies do not yet establish the accumulated effect on a whole socio-technical system.

This is a transferability and revalidation claim, not a claim that Rasmussen's framework or all earlier human-factors research is obsolete. The framework tells us what to examine when technology changes the behaviour-shaping conditions.

## Accuracy guardrails

- Rasmussen's 1997 paper predates LLMs and provides a framework for inquiry, not evidence about their effects.
- Current studies show heterogeneous task-level effects. Do not generalize them to every worker, task, or organization.
- The CHI 2025 study is a survey of self-reported behaviour and associations, not a causal workplace experiment.
- Revalidate established models and measures in the changed context; do not discard the human-factors literature.
- Treat AI-mediated adaptation as reciprocal. Tools alter work conditions, while repeated human adaptations alter workflows, expectations, and later decisions.
