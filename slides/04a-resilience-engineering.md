## Speaker notes


The working definition is this talk's synthesis. Allspaw's 6 March 2019 abstract supplies background on investment in adaptive capacity, including how engineering teams anticipate, handle and learn from unexpected situations.

Woods describes resilience through capabilities available before surprise calls on them. Cook describes practitioners and first-line management continually adjusting work, resources and recovery options. These perspectives explain why people, knowledge, coordination and practical room to act belong within the system being discussed.

**Surprise and human adaptation**

David D. Woods: “Resilience concerns the capabilities a system needs to respond to inevitable surprises.” [Resilience is a Verb (2018)](https://beta.irgc.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/12/Woods-for-IRGC-Resilience-Guide-Vol-2-2018.pdf), p. 1.

Richard I. Cook: “Human practitioners are the adaptable element of complex systems.” [How Complex Systems Fail](https://www.adaptivecapacitylabs.com/HowComplexSystemsFail.pdf), point 12, p. 3 (1998, revised 21 April 2000).

This definition introduces the capability the later boundary and control examples examine. It does not promise uninterrupted service or unlimited capacity to absorb pressure.
