# Slide 9 — Diagram Legend

## Slide copy

**Diagram Legend**

- 💸 **Economic boundary** — Where money runs out
- 😢 **Workload boundary** — Where people cannot keep up
- 💥 **Performance boundary** — Where the system stops working acceptably
- ··· **Error margin** — Buffer before the performance limit
- 🔴 **Operating point** — Where the whole system is now
- 🟢 **Playing surface** — Room within the boundaries
- Tank + ✋ + 📉 **Actions and pressures** — Local actions and external pressures can change the operating point or its constraints.

## Speaker notes

Recap the symbols used in the Fish diagram. The operating point represents the current state of the whole socio-technical system, not a person. The dotted line marks a perceived limit inside the actual performance boundary; the gap between them is the error margin. Boundaries constrain the playing surface, but they do not push the point in any direction.

The combined tank, hand and chart are this talk's shorthand for actions and pressures: safety interventions, adaptations to reduce effort, and pressure toward cost efficiency. Rasmussen's Figure 3 describes local adaptive variations, effort and cost gradients, and a safety countergradient. Figure 1 shows technological, market and political stressors acting across the socio-technical hierarchy. Local actions and wider pressures interact; an external condition is not necessarily a deliberate action. In this adaptation, changes can shift the operating point or reshape the constraints around it.

Actions may arise within a team's immediate work or elsewhere in the control hierarchy. Management and regulators remain part of the wider socio-technical system; environmental stressors act across it.
