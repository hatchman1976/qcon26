# Slide 13 — AI and the Workload Boundary

## Slide copy

**AI and the workload boundary**

Cheaper generation can create more checking work.

_Recurring theme rail: Economic / **Workload** / Performance / Error Margin._

Starting state:

- **Demand:** Count review, integration and maintenance.

Reveal 1:

- **Capacity:** Preserve focused attention and context.

Reveal 2:

- **Response:** Change scope, timing or admitted work.

Reveal 3:

**Match admitted work to verification capacity.**

## Speaker notes

The economic slide asks where costs go. Here, follow the work: generating a candidate quickly does not establish that someone can verify, integrate or maintain it quickly. Chapter 5 §5.3.1 describes filtering and revision costs even in ordinary work. Sections 5.3.2–5.3.4 add a difficulty: plausible mistakes can demand expertise to recognize and correct. The amount of checking varies by task, output, workflow and expertise.

Count admitted work and the focused verification effort it requires. A reviewer assigned to many tasks can still lack the time or context to assess this one. The talk's review-load ratio is an illustrative capacity heuristic, not a measure established by the chapter. Rising demand relative to effective capacity can increase backlog and delay; defects additionally depend on how the organization adapts to that pressure.

Protect contextual knowledge, source evidence and practical authority to change commitments. The chapter's safety-manager story illustrates responsibility without adequate resources; it is a scenario, not a measured universal outcome. A named approver needs enough time to inspect the evidence and a usable way to reject, pause or reverse action.

Respond by limiting admitted work, changing scope or accepting latency; improve verification capacity where the tools actually help. AI can support tests and diagnosis as well as generation. Holding output fixed while removing expertise or thinning review can transfer economic pressure into workload and performance. That is a conditional mechanism, not an inevitable effect of AI.
