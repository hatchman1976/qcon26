## Slide copy and sequence

**Economic Boundary Actions**

Signals become decisions that push toward cost efficiency.

| Leading signal / metric | Possible management action |
| --- | --- |
| Forecast cash burn ↑<br />Runway ↓ | Reduce scope; defer investment;<br />reprioritize commitments |
| Cost per verified outcome ↑ | Renegotiate suppliers; rightsize resources;<br />remove low-value work |
| Idle capacity or unused licences ↑ | Consolidate platforms; retire unused capacity;<br />check redundancy before cutting |
| Projected margin ↓ | Freeze hiring; reduce staffing or maintenance<br />**→ savings can transfer risk** |

The gradient points toward cost efficiency.<br />Watch for lost assurance on the performance side.

## Speaker notes

A forecast is a signal and a budget is a target. An actor with authority decides what to do. These are possible organizational responses, not recommendations to reduce staffing or resilience. Unit economics should include human verification and recovery. Right-sizing and retiring unused services can preserve performance; cutting necessary expertise, maintenance or redundancy while retaining commitments can consume performance margin. Validate the effect of savings across the whole workflow. Existing incidents or realized losses are lagging outcomes; forecast cash and projected margins are earlier financial signals.

**Account for transferred work**

Chapter 5 §5.2.4 (printed p. 81; supplied PDF p. 89) asks who receives a saving, who absorbs the work, and when it becomes visible. Track authoring savings beside reviewer, maintainer and on-call costs. Section 5.5.2 (printed p. 85; PDF p. 93) extends evaluation to future adaptability, coupling, congestion and conflict. A local saving is not sufficient evidence of a net workflow saving.

Financial feedback must reach people with authority, competence and resources to change commitments. They must communicate effects on review demand, maintenance and recovery across levels. Savings preserve other margins when low-value work is removed. If output stays fixed while expertise or review are cut, the resulting adaptations can transfer pressure into workload and acceptable performance.
