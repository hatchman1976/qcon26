## Slide copy and sequence

**Economic Boundary Actions**

Signals become decisions that push toward cost efficiency.

Leading signal / metric

Possible management action

Forecast cash burn ↑ / Runway ↓

Reduce scope; defer investment; / reprioritize commitments

Cost per verified outcome ↑

Renegotiate suppliers; rightsize resources; / remove low-value work

Idle capacity or unused licences ↑

Consolidate platforms; retire unused capacity; / check redundancy before cutting

Projected margin ↓

Freeze hiring; reduce staffing or maintenance / → savings can transfer risk

The gradient points toward cost efficiency. / Watch for lost assurance on the performance side.

Model: Rasmussen (1997), Figure 3 · Signals and responses: illustrative, not automatic rules.

## Speaker notes

A forecast is a signal and a budget is a target. An actor with authority decides what to do. These are possible organizational responses, not recommendations to reduce staffing or resilience. Unit economics should include human verification and recovery. Right-sizing and retiring unused services can preserve performance; cutting necessary expertise, maintenance or redundancy while retaining commitments can consume performance margin. Validate the effect of savings across the whole workflow. Existing incidents or realized losses are lagging outcomes; forecast cash and projected margins are earlier financial signals.
