# Slide 11 — AI Enters the Control Structure

## Slide copy

**AI enters the control structure**

It changes the path from evidence to action.

Starting state:

- **Controllers:** goals · criteria · authority
- **Feedback:** observations and outcomes
- **Control:** decisions and actions
- **Work system:** actual operating state

Reveal 1:

- **AI:** lower effort
- **Gather:** find and frame evidence
- **Offer options:** shape what gets considered
- **Draft action:** turn intent into output

Reveal 2:

- **Plausible ≠ measured**
- **Fluent ≠ intended**

Reveal 3:

- The actual state has moved closer to the performance boundary
- The controller's delayed picture remains farther inside the perceived boundary

Reveal 4:

**AI can make “generate, accept, act” the path of least effort.**

**If verification does not keep pace, enacted decisions can consume margin before the control structure sees the change.**

> “No control system will perform better than its measuring channel.”
>
> Jens Rasmussen, 1997

## Speaker notes

Rasmussen's control structure is the distributed network of decision makers, goals, constraints, information, competence, and authority that controls the work. AI is not automatically a new controller and it is not a fourth boundary. It enters the channels that existing controllers use.

On the feedback side, AI can gather information, summarize evidence, find contradictions, and support testing. It can also compress uncertainty into a fluent account that looks more directly observed than it really is.

On the control side, AI can present options, draft decisions, and turn loosely stated intent into detailed output. That can increase useful capacity. It can also amplify a preference beyond what the communicator intended or let plausible wording substitute for explicit criteria and verification.

“Least effort” is not a moral claim about laziness. It describes normal local adaptation under competing goals and limited attention. AI output does not move the system merely by existing. The operating state moves when people or automation admit and enact the resulting decisions.

This risk is conditional. AI can strengthen the safety counter-gradient when it improves testing, diagnosis, challenge, and feedback. Risk rises when generation and action become easier faster than assurance and correction. Then the actual operating state can move closer to a functional limit while the control structure's picture of the system remains behind.

Rasmussen's 1997 paper describes behaviour and work activity migrating toward the boundary. “Operating point” is later shorthand used explicitly by Cook and Rasmussen in 2005.
