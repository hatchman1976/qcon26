# Resilience Engineering: definition and quotation sources

Primary sources checked on 27 October 2026 for [Resilience Engineering](../slides/04a-resilience-engineering.md), immediately after the socio-technical systems introduction. The visible definition is the talk's synthesis. It concerns building capabilities that help a socio-technical system continue functioning as circumstances change, without claiming a guarantee of uninterrupted operation.

## Background source: John Allspaw

[Amplifying Sources of Resilience](https://speakerdeck.com/jallspaw/amplifying-sources-of-resilience), John Allspaw, published 6 March 2019. The Speaker Deck abstract supports the [working definition](../slides/04a-resilience-engineering.md) as background, with no quotation or attribution on the visible slide.

The abstract connects investment in adaptive capacity with engineering teams' ability to anticipate, respond and learn when preventive designs do not cover the situation. Use it to introduce the field's focus, rather than as empirical proof of a particular resilience intervention.

## Optional preparation quotation: David D. Woods

[Resilience is a Verb](https://beta.irgc.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/12/Woods-for-IRGC-Resilience-Guide-Vol-2-2018.pdf), David D. Woods, 2018, in the *IRGC Resource Guide on Resilience*, volume 2, p. 1.

> Resilience concerns the capabilities a system needs to respond to inevitable surprises.

This 12-word option emphasises readiness before an event demands action. Woods discusses anticipating trouble, coordinating roles, mobilising responses and learning about how systems become brittle. Adaptive capacity can be developed, sustained or diminished. Keep this option in speaker preparation rather than on the slide.

## Optional preparation quotation: Richard I. Cook

[How Complex Systems Fail](https://www.adaptivecapacitylabs.com/HowComplexSystemsFail.pdf), Richard I. Cook, 1998, revision D dated 21 April 2000, point 12, p. 3.

> Human practitioners are the adaptable element of complex systems.

This nine-word option introduces people's contribution to adaptation. The accompanying passage describes practitioners and first-line managers reconfiguring work, directing scarce resources, preparing recovery routes and noticing changed performance. It is a statement about their role within a complex system, not a claim that people have unlimited capacity or that technical design is irrelevant. Keep it in speaker preparation rather than on the slide.
