The dot moves. The edges can move too.

Plain-language script: “This dot is not a person. It is the whole system — people, software, and rules — working right now. It keeps moving as work changes. I am going to draw three chalk lines around it. The first marks where the system can no longer do its job acceptably or safely. The second marks where we can no longer pay the bills. The third marks where people cannot keep going. We cannot see the performance line exactly, so we work from where we think it is. The gap between our perceived line and the real limit is our margin for error. Everyone makes sensible choices. The business asks for cheaper and faster. People make work easier. Together, those pressures can move the dot toward the performance edge. There is no magic safe spot in the middle. The lines do not push the dot back. Any line can move as demand, staffing, technology, or AI changes. That is Rasmussen's warning: failure does not require one stupid decision. Normal adaptations can slowly move the system toward an edge nobody can see exactly.” Adult one-sentence version: one current operating state moves within economic, workload, and acceptable-performance constraints; pressures move it, while sensing and control preserve margin and recoverability.
